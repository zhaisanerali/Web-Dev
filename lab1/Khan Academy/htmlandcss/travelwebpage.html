Indonesia is a vast Southeast Asian country made up of over 17,000 islands, known for its incredible diversity of cultures, landscapes, and wildlife. From the bustling streets of Jakarta to the serene rice terraces of Bali, Indonesia offers something for every traveler. The country is famous for its tropical beaches, active volcanoes, ancient temples, and rich traditions. With thousands of islands to explore, visitors can enjoy surfing, diving, hiking, and experiencing unique local cultures, making Indonesia one of the world’s most exotic and exciting destinations.

Places to visit

  1. Komodo Islands

    2. Komodo Island is one of the only places in the world where you can spot Komodo dragons—the world’s largest lizards—in their natural environment. On this tour, set sail from Labuan Bajo and spend a day exploring the islands of the Komodo National Park. Head ashore to see the Komodo dragons on Komodo Island, trek around the volcanic landscapes of Padar Island, and enjoy multiple stops to swim and snorkel including Manta Point and Pink Beach.

  2. Dreamland Beach

    3. Dreamland Beach, located in the Pecatu area of Bali, is a stunning destination known for its golden sand, crystal-clear waters, and dramatic limestone cliffs. This beach has a unique charm, combining natural beauty with a laid-back vibe, making it popular among surfers and sunbathers alike. The waves here are ideal for surfing, especially for intermediate and advanced surfers, while the broad shoreline is perfect for relaxing and enjoying the tropical sun.

  3. Raja Ampat Island

    4. Raja Ampat is an archipelago in Indonesia known for its breathtaking natural beauty and incredible marine biodiversity. Often called a diver’s paradise, it features crystal-clear turquoise waters, vibrant coral reefs, and over 1,500 species of fish. The islands are remote and largely untouched, offering pristine beaches, hidden lagoons, and dramatic limestone cliffs. Visitors can enjoy snorkeling, diving, island-hopping, and exploring local Papuan culture. Raja Ampat is perfect for travelers seeking adventure, peace, and an unforgettable tropical escape.

  4. Borobudur Temple

    5. Borobudur Temple Compounds This famous Buddhist temple, dating from the 8th and 9th centuries, is located in central Java. It was built in three tiers: a pyramidal base with five concentric square terraces, the trunk of a cone with three circular platforms and, at the top, a monumental stupa. The walls and balustrades are decorated with fine low reliefs, covering a total surface area of 2,500 m2. Around the circular platforms are 72 openwork stupas, each containing a statue of the Buddha. The monument was restored with UNESCO's help in the 1970s.

  5. Mount Bromo

    6. Mt Bromo is undoubtedly on of the most popular and well known tourist attractions of East Java Province. The pre-dawn departure and trek across the mountain's famous 'sand sea', to watch the sunrise at the crater rim, has become something of a ritual, enacted daily by people of every nationality. Bromo is actually just one crater in the vast, 800 km² Tengger massif, which forms the largest of East Java's five main volcanic ranges. Although by no means the highest mountain in the region, it has gained its reputation partly because of its unique location and partly through the reverence shown to it by the local inhabitants. The Tengger Range is one of eight official nature reserves in East Java and centres around the peaks of Mts. Bromo and Semeru. Most of the area is more than 2000 metres above sea level, Mt Semeru itself being Java's highest mountain at 3,676m.   6. Tanjung Puting National Park

    7. Nestled in the heart of Indonesian Borneo, Tanjung Puting National Park beckons adventurers with its lush rainforests, meandering rivers, and diverse wildlife. A boat tour through this iconic national park offers a unique opportunity to immerse oneself in the natural beauty and biodiversity of the region. As one of the largest protected areas in Southeast Asia, Tanjung Puting is renowned for its population of critically endangered orangutans, making it a must-visit destination for wildlife enthusiasts and nature lovers alike. The park's dense tropical forests provide a sanctuary for these magnificent creatures, along with a myriad of other wildlife species, including proboscis monkeys, gibbons, and exotic bird species. Embarking on a boat tour along the park's winding rivers, such as the Sekonyer River, allows visitors to traverse deep into the heart of the jungle while enjoying panoramic views of the surrounding landscape.

  7. Bangka Belitung Islands

    8. Find the History of Bangka at the Pangkal Pinang Tin Museum The Tin Museum was officially opened on August 2, 1997. This museum displays the history of tin mining on Bangka Island which contains historical objects and tin mining dioramas. In addition, visitors can also watch the making of crafts from tin. The best part is that admission to the museum is free. In addition, if you want to find food around Bangka Belitung, there are many places to eat that you can choose from. The dining options available in Bangka-Belitung will satisfy any hungry traveler. After visiting the Tin Museum, you can head to Kampung Bintang Seafood Restaurant located in Bintang Dalam District or Asui Seafood Restaurant on Jalan Bintang to find delicious seafood. You can also try Tan Kasteel Restaurant in Pangkal Pinang, near the museum.

  8. Lake Toba

    9. On a fertile volcanic plateau that covers much of northern Central Sumatra, lies the lovely Lake Toba, a vast crater lake containing the lush Samosir island (nearly the size of Singapore). The result of a great prehistoric eruption, Lake Toba is one of the highest (900 metres/2,900ft) and deepest (450 metres/1,480ft) lakes on earth.

  9. Ubud

    10. Inland Ubud and the surrounding area form Bali’s cultural heartland. Ubud is home to a huge number of temples, museums and art galleries. Next to that, it's one of Asia’s leading yoga centres. Its wealth of craft studios offer some absorbing shopping. Ubud also hosts nightly Balinese dance shows and it's a great place to see traditional ceremonies and daily rituals, too. Ubud’s lovely location becomes even prettier beyond the main centre. Set amid lush, terraced rice paddies, there’s plenty of scope for hikes and bicycle rides. For a sense of traditional Bali, visit the arty Penestanan or the Bronze Age relics of Pejeng.
